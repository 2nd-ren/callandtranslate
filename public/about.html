About

Someone to make the call when you don’t speak their language.

Call & Translate is for people who need something done at the other end of a phone line, and the person who can do it doesn’t speak their language. You brief the app as you would brief a person who works for you. It talks to them in their language. You stay on speakerphone, follow along in yours, and keep a written record when you hang up.

The job it does

You tell it what you need to get done and give it everything it should take into the call: names, dates, account numbers, addresses, the questions you want answered, the outcome you want. It then acts as if you had a personal translator — and a person who actually gets things done — sitting with you on speakerphone.

It already has the information the other side is likely to ask for. It already has the information you want to find out. It talks to them in their language. You hear the call, and in real time you see a transcript plus a translation into your own language.

You stay on the line

This is not a black box that goes away and calls someone for you. You place the call, put the phone on speaker, and start the agent. All three of you can hear each other: you, the agent, and the person on the other end.

Because you can follow the transcript, you can interrupt. Add a question. Correct a name. Change the request. The agent conveys what you said in their language and continues. The conversation stays natural, and nothing important is missed because you were “out of the loop.”

When they ask for something it doesn’t have

If the other person asks for a fact that is not in the brief, the agent does not invent it. It tells them, in their language, that it will check with you. Then it asks you in your language. You answer on speaker. It takes that answer back to them in theirs.

That is the difference between a translation gadget and someone who can actually finish the call. The other side is not left hanging. You are not guessing. The record shows exactly what was asked and what was given.

How you prepare

Before the call you write the brief in your language:

  • What you need done — the goal, in plain words.
  • Facts they will ask for — identity, booking details, account notes, constraints.
  • What you want to find out — hours, price, availability, a confirmation number, a yes or no.
  • How to handle the call — who you are calling on behalf of, how direct to be, how to close.

Write it in your own words, the way you would brief a person. The structured fields exist so you do not forget the one detail they will definitely ask for. Save it as a template if you make the same kind of call more than once.

What happens during the call

  1. You set your language and theirs, and choose the agent voice.
  2. You dial the number on your phone and put it on speaker.
  3. The agent speaks to them in their language, working the brief.
  4. A live transcript appears. Each line is translated into your language underneath.
  5. You can jump in at any time. The agent translates and continues.
  6. If they ask for missing information, the agent checks with you, then answers them.

Speakerphone is deliberate. The other person can hear that you are there. That honesty reduces the hesitation of “I’m only talking to a machine,” and it means you can take over a sentence without anyone being surprised.

What you keep afterwards

When the call ends, the transcript is saved with the session. You can generate a report that covers:

  • What was said
  • What was done — booked, confirmed, refused, deferred
  • What was learned and what information was captured
  • Anything still outstanding — a callback, a document, a number they didn’t have

That report is the thing you forward to a colleague, keep for a landlord, or use when you walk into the pharmacy an hour later.

Who it is for

People who are new in a country, or on holiday

The practical calls: a hotel that needs to extend a stay, a clinic that needs a date of birth, a town hall appointment, a restaurant, delayed luggage, a landlord, a missed connection. You should not have to wait until you find a bilingual friend.

Companies that only sometimes need another language

A supplier in another country, a venue, a local office, a customer who called in their language. You do not always have a translator on staff, and you should not have to stall the work until one is free. Brief the agent with the file, make the call, keep the report.

Why not a traditional translator

Traditional interpretation is often the wrong shape for this problem. It usually needs scheduling, a separate briefing, and a person on the clock. That is right for a court hearing or a long negotiation. It is too much setup for “can you tell me if the parcel arrived” or “please change the booking to Friday.”

Call & Translate is built for those calls: short, practical, high-stakes enough that you cannot mime your way through them, and urgent enough that you need to do them now.

What it is not

It is not a set-and-forget robot that phones people while you leave the room. You are on the call. You can hear them. You can stop it. You can correct it. That is the product.
